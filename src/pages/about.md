I'm a writer at heart, and always will be–a technical writer who appreciates the role of science and technology in society, and wants to help people [use it correctly](https://everypageispageone.com/2014/02/10/the-goal-of-tech-comm-is-to-enable-correct-action/). [I've long believed](https://richardrabil.com/2017/10/20/get-into-technical-writing/) that a culture like ours needs talented, conscientious communicators who can write clearly and effectively about what we build. That's one reason why, after finishing my BA in professional writing, I went on to complete my master's degree in technical communication.

## A Systems Mindset

Ever since entering the field I have also been interested in *structure*: how to organize and design documentation / knowledge systems so they can grow in a sustainable way. I'm constantly asking systems-oriented questions such as:

* How do we scale this content after publishing the first version? How should it be organized to grow and expand over time as the product or service evolves? What if we need to migrate to a different repository or format?
* What does the next writer or editor who needs to maintain this documentation in the future need to know? What style guidance should they use? What templates and content rules should they follow? What experts should they rely on for review?
* How do we track the health of the enormous volumes of information under our responsibility? When and how do we need retire information when it is no longer necessary?

These questions have led me into many overlapping disciplines, from technical editing and information architecture to content strategy and knowledge management. I have frequently taken on "team lead" and "architect" roles for large-scale documentation projects in the organizations I work for: setting up governance frameworks, designing information models, defining the content strategy for new efforts and team collaborators (consisting of full-time writers as well as non-writer contributors), and sharing best practices and articles about the latest trends in the field.

## Where AI Comes In

The explosive growth of AI has taken my systems thinking to a new level. In addition to researching and publishing user-friendly docs, I now regularly apply my systems thinking and editorial judgment to develop prompts, skills, agents, and workflows to help teams generate and publish content consistently and at scale. The questions I keep encountering are: Where can we use the strengths of AI to elevate documentation and better serve end users, in ways that are sustainable across individuals with different needs and ways of working? And how we can be sure that the "fuel" which powers AI–the underlying knowledge and documentation–is as healthy as it can be so that users trust it?

This combination–seasoned technical writer and AI-augmented knowledge architect–is what I can bring to your organization.
